## T1: Schwartz Space S(ℝⁿ) as Nuclear Fréchet Space

### What it is
The Schwartz space $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ consists of all $C^\infty$ functions $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{C}$ such that for all multi-indices $\alpha, \beta$:
$$\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} |x^\alpha \partial^\beta \varphi(x)| < \infty$$

These are the "rapidly decreasing smooth functions" — the natural domain for tempered distributions.

### Why we need it
The entire Gelfand triple $\Phi \subset L^2 \subset \Phi'$ depends on $\Phi = \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (or a variant like $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for compact support). Without the Schwartz space, we cannot define:
- The space $\Phi'$ where distributions live (DIST-1)
- The duality pairing $\langle T, \varphi \rangle$ (DIST-2)
- The Sobolev regularity classification (DIST-3, DIST-5)

## T2: Tempered Distributions S'(ℝⁿ) as Inductive-Limit Dual

### What it is
$\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the continuous dual of the Schwartz space. Its topology is the **strong dual topology**, which is an inductive limit of Banach spaces (the LF-space structure):
$$\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n) = \bigcup_{k \in \mathbb{N}} \mathcal{S}'_k(\mathbb{R}^n)$$
where $\mathcal{S}'_k$ are the distributions of order ≤ k.

### Why we need it
- All distribution objects live in $\Phi' = \mathcal{S}'$ (distributions, measures SRB, Dirac deltas)
- The DIST-7 stability proof **critically depends** on the inductive-limit structure
- The duality pairing $\langle\cdot, \cdot\rangle: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{S}' \to \mathbb{R}$ is the fundamental bilinear form

### What needs to be built in Mathlib

## T3: Schwartz Structure Theorem

### What it is
Every tempered distribution $T$ of finite order $k$ with compact support $K$ can be represented as a **finite sum of derivatives of continuous functions**:
$$T = \sum_{|\alpha| \leq k} \partial^\alpha f_\alpha$$
where each $f_\alpha$ is a continuous function with support contained in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $K$.

### Why we need it
**This is the single most critical theorem.** Without it:
- DIST-1 (dual representation well-definedness) cannot be proven
- DIST-7 (stability under perturbations) cannot move beyond Sobolev norms to $\Phi'$
- The connection between the algebraic singularity representation and the abstract distribution is broken
- The inductive-limit reduction (Lemma 3 of the stability proof) depends on it

### What needs to be built in Mathlib

```lean4
-- 1. Statement

  (T : TemperedDistribution ℝⁿ)
  (h_order : order T ≤ k)  -- T has finite order k
  (h_support : support T ⊆ K) (hK : IsCompact K) :
  ∀ ε > 0, ∃ (f : (Fin n → ℕ) → C(ℝⁿ, ℝ)),  -- f_α indexed by multi-indices
    (∀ α, support (f α) ⊆ thickening ε K) ∧
    (T = ∑_{|α|≤k} (∂^α (f α))) := ...

-- 2. Constructive version: given a dual representation, extract the f_α
def decompose_distribution (R : DualRepresentation) : 
  Σ' (f : (Fin n → ℕ) → C(ℝⁿ, ℝ)), 
    R.as_distribution = ∑_{|α| ≤ R.max_order} ∂^α (f α) := ...

-- 3. Regular part / singular part decomposition
theorem regular_singular_decomposition
  (T : TemperedDistribution ℝⁿ) (h_order : order T ≤ k) :
  ∃ (T_reg T_sing : TemperedDistribution ℝⁿ),
    T = T_reg + T_sing ∧
    (∀ φ, T_reg φ = ∫ f_reg · φ) ∧  -- T_reg is integration against a smooth function
    (T_sing = finite_sum_of_dirac_derivatives) := ...
```

### What needs to be developed specifically

The proof requires several intermediate results:

1. **Riesz representation for distributions of order 0:** Distributions of order 0 are Radon measures
2. **Smoothing operators:** Convolution with mollifiers to approximate distributions by smooth functions
3. **Local representation:** On compact sets, every distribution is a finite-order derivative of a continuous function
4. **Partition of unity:** Glue local representations into a global one (uses sheaf property)

## T4: Wavefront Set WF(T) ⊂ T*X\{0}

### What it is
The wavefront set $WF(T)$ of a distribution $T$ is a closed conic subset of the cotangent bundle $T^*X \setminus \{0\}$ that encodes both:
- Where $T$ is singular (projection onto $X$ gives the singular support)
- In which directions the singularity propagates (the "high-frequency" behavior)

A point $(x_0, \xi_0) \in T^*X \setminus \{0\}$ is NOT in $WF(T)$ if there exists a cutoff function $\chi$ with $\chi(x_0) \neq 0$ such that the Fourier transform $\widehat{\chi T}(\xi)$ decays rapidly in a conic neighborhood of $\xi_0$.

### Why we need it
- DIST-4 (Hörmander product condition) requires $WF(T_1) \cap N(WF(T_2)) = \emptyset$
- The microlocal extension of the dual representation uses $WF(T)$ directly
- Convolution, product, and restriction of distributions all depend on wavefront set analysis
- The condition $(x, \xi) \in WF(T), (x, -\xi) \in WF(T)$ signals an obstruction to well-definedness

### What needs to be built in Mathlib


## T5: Hörmander Product Theorem

### What it is
For two distributions $T_1, T_2$, the product $T_1 \cdot T_2$ exists as a distribution if and only if their wavefront sets satisfy:
$$WF(T_1) \cap \{(x, -\xi) : (x, \xi) \in WF(T_2)\} = \emptyset$$

This is Hörmander's criterion for multiplication of distributions (Hörmander 1971, Theorem 8.2.10). The antipodal map $N: (x, \xi) \mapsto (x, -\xi)$ ensures that the pointwise product of their Fourier transforms converges.

### Why we need it
- DIST-4 (convolution well-definedness) depends on this theorem
- `AlgebraicConvolver._verify_hormander_exact()` checks this condition computationally
- Without it, we cannot guarantee that our convolution operations are mathematically legitimate
- The entire microlocal approach to distribution theory depends on this criterion

### What needs to be built in Mathlib


## T6: Sobolev Embedding Constants for Finite Domains

### What it is
Sobolev embedding theorems state that $H^s(\Omega) \subset C^k(\overline{\Omega})$ when $s > k + d/2$, where $d$ is the dimension and $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary.

What we need specifically: **explicit, computable constants** $C_{\text{Sob}}(k, \Omega)$ such that:
$$\|\varphi\|_{C^k(\overline{\Omega})} \leq C_{\text{Sob}}(k, \Omega) \cdot \|\varphi\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}$$

For our 1D domain $[-1, 1]$, the constant can be computed explicitly using the reproducing kernel of $H^1$ (Green's function).

### Why we need it
- Lemma 1 of the DIST-7 proof: $\|T_\eta - T\|_{H^{-(k+1)}} \leq C_1 \cdot |\eta|$
- DIST-3 (differentiation exactness): error bound from Chebyshev projection
- DIST-5 (order truncation): convergence rate depends on Sobolev embedding

### What needs to be built in Mathlib

```lean4

  (a b : ℝ) (h : a < b) (k : ℕ) :
  ∃ (C : ℝ≥0), ∀ (φ : H^{k+1} (Icc a b)),
    ‖φ‖_{C^k(Icc a b)} ≤ C * ‖φ‖_{H^{k+1}(Icc a b)} := ...

-- 2. Trace theorem for point evaluation

  |φ x₀| ≤ C_trace * ‖φ‖_{H¹} := ...

-- 3. Dual Sobolev norm of Dirac

  ‖dirac x₀‖_{H^s} ≤ C_dirac(s) := ...

-- 4. Specific constants for domain [-1, 1]
```

## T7: Čech Cohomology with Distribution Coefficients (Soft Sheaf Property)

### What it is
Distributions form a **soft sheaf**: every section on a closed set extends to the whole space, and every compatible family of local sections glues to a unique global section. This is the abstract version of the cohomological gluing protocol.

The key statement: for any open cover $\{U_i\}$ of a domain $\Omega$, the Čech cohomology group $\check{H}^1(\{U_i\}, \mathcal{D}')$ is trivial, where $\mathcal{D}'$ is the sheaf of distributions.

### Why we need it
- DIST-6 (cohomological gluing ⇒ global consistency)
- The entire cohomological gluing protocol (`CohomologicalGluingProtocol`) is a computational realization of this sheaf property
- Guarantees that $H^1 = 0$ for the sheaf of distributions (softness)

### What needs to be built in Mathlib

